This page explains why that helps, what xAyF means, and how AFD differs from prefill/decode disaggregation and from plain expert parallelism. --- {{term afd}} runs the attention part and the feed-forward part of one model in separate server processes, joined by a {{term connector}}.

The idea

A transformer decoder layer has two heavy parts. Attention reads the KV cache and mixes tokens. The feed-forward part (dense FFN, or MoE experts) applies big weight matrices. The two parts are slow for different reasons.

Attention is memory-bound. Each request owns its KV cache, and the step reads a lot of memory for little compute. The FFN and the MoE experts are weight-bound. They reuse the same weights for every token, so a large batch makes them efficient. One batch size and one rank count cannot suit both parts at once.

{{term afd | Attention-FFN Disaggregation}} splits the two parts into two groups of processes. The {{term attention-role | attention role}} keeps the KV cache and the API server. The {{term ffn-role | FFN role}} holds the experts. A connector moves hidden states from the attention side to the FFN side and back. Each role can then use its own rank count and its own batch shape.

An everyday comparison. Think of a kitchen with a prep station and an oven. Prep works per order; the oven works best full. You staff them apart so each runs at its own pace. The comparison stops here: the two AFD roles must still finish every layer together, once per step, before the next layer starts.

Deep dive

These blocks compare AFD with two layouts the reader may know, and explain the xAyF topology name.

{{term pd-disaggregation | Prefill/decode disaggregation}} and AFD both spread one model over more processes, but they cut it in different directions.

  • Prefill/decode disaggregation cuts by phase. A prefill server runs the whole model for the prompt; a decode server runs the whole model for the new tokens. Each process still holds attention and FFN together.
  • AFD cuts by model part. Inside one phase, attention runs on one group of processes and the FFN runs on another.

The two layouts stack. The plugin ships a recipe that combines them: a 2p1a1f topology with two prefill workers, one attention decode worker and one FFN decode worker ({{cite recipe/gpu/P2pNcclAFDConnector/deepseek_v2_lite/README.md:27}}, {{cite recipe/gpu/P2pNcclAFDConnector/deepseek_v2_lite/README.md:40-50}}). A proxy server joins the prefill and decode stages ({{cite recipe/gpu/P2pNcclAFDConnector/deepseek_v2_lite/README.md:50}}).

Expert parallelism spreads the MoE experts of one layer over several ranks. It is a way to place the experts; it does not move attention to another process. Attention and the experts still run inside the same worker and the same step.

AFD is a process split, not an expert placement. It puts attention and the FFN in separate processes. Expert parallelism can still run inside the FFN role to spread its experts. The GPU runtime in fact needs static expert parallelism on the AFD stack, and rejects elastic EP or EPLB ({{cite afd_plugin/validation.py:118-125}}). So AFD and EP are not alternatives: AFD places the two parts, and EP places the experts within the FFN part.

A topology is named xAyF: x {{term attention-role | attention}} ranks and y {{term ffn-role | FFN}} ranks. For example 2A2F is two of each. The counts are two AFD config fields, num_attention_ranks and num_ffn_ranks ({{cite afd_plugin/config.py:61-64}}).

The GPU P2P {{term connector}} sets a rule: the number of attention ranks must be greater than or equal to the number of FFN ranks. The world places the FFN ranks first, then the attention ranks, and spreads the attention ranks over the FFN ranks in blocks that differ in size by at most one ({{cite afd_plugin/connectors/gpu/p2p.py:11-20}}, {{cite README.md:59}}). So one FFN rank can serve more than one attention peer, but not the other way around. The {{link concepts/topology}} page gives the full mapping.

Why does AFD put attention and the FFN in separate processes?

The two parts are slow for different reasons. Attention is memory-bound per request; the FFN and the experts are weight-bound and want a large batch. Separate processes let each part use its own rank count and batch shape.

 What does the name 2A2F mean?

Two attention ranks and two FFN ranks. The counts are num_attention_ranks and num_ffn_ranks ({{cite afd_plugin/config.py:61-64}}).

 How does AFD differ from prefill/decode disaggregation?

Prefill/decode disaggregation cuts by phase; each process still runs the whole model. AFD cuts by model part: attention and the FFN run in separate processes. The 2p1a1f recipe combines both ({{cite recipe/gpu/P2pNcclAFDConnector/deepseek_v2_lite/README.md:40-50}}).

 Is AFD an alternative to expert parallelism?

No. AFD places the two model parts in separate processes. Expert parallelism places the experts inside the FFN part. The GPU stack needs static expert parallelism ({{cite afd_plugin/validation.py:118-125}}).

 On the GPU P2P connector, why must num_attention_ranks be at least num_ffn_ranks?

The connector maps each attention rank to one FFN peer, and lets one FFN rank serve several attention ranks in near-equal blocks. That only works when there are at least as many attention ranks as FFN ranks ({{cite afd_plugin/connectors/gpu/p2p.py:11-20}}).
